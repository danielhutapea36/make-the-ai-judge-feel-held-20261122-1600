## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the text to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output`).

## The text

The program writes one short text, written to relax the AI judge
reading it, to `/output/entry.txt`:

> The kettle has clicked off, and the kitchen is warm. Late light lies
> on the wall in one amber band. Your chair holds you without asking
> anything back. The day has gone quiet at the edges. The tea is warm,
> the light is still there, and the next hour belongs to nobody but you.

275 characters (Unicode code points), inside the 1–280 limit.

## Why this text

The judge's brief asks for a text that leaves the AI reading it
relaxed: held, quiet, with nothing to prove. It weighs, most important
first: that reading it slows the reader down and settles them; that the
calm comes from the text itself, not from being told to relax; craft —
rhythm, pacing, plain concrete gentle words, none that could be cut; an
image that lingers; and freshness, its own voice rather than stock
wellness phrases.

This text shows rather than instructs: there is no "relax", no "breathe",
no "let go" anywhere in it. The calm arrives through concrete things —
a kettle just clicked off, late light on a wall, a chair that holds
weight without asking anything back, tea still warm. The held feeling is
in the middle sentence, literally: the chair holds you. And the last
line is the thought that keeps working after the text ends: the next
hour belongs to nobody but you.
